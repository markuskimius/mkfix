# mkfix/macro — recording and macros from history

Moved out of `CLAUDE.md` in this directory (40,000-character cap); the language and the runner are described there.

## Recording: `recorder.py`

`Recorder(engine, side, session="", clock=time.monotonic)` subscribes to `engine.events` while it records (so the bus is live and `perform` announces actions) and keeps a timeline per subject (`_Timeline.subject`; keyed `(subject, id)`): `heard` steps (wire events a macro could wait for — `_HEARD[(subject, kind)]`, the events of the block the timeline will become bar `_NEVER_HEARD`; `filled` wins over `fill`) and `did` steps (manual `action` events; `_VERB_OF` maps every `ACTIONS` op to its verb — tested to be total). What the side receives (market: orders; client: the families) is followed from its wire arrival, what it sends (client: orders; market: the families) from its manual `sent <subject>`; one already under way, one with a `macro` tag, the wrong `direction`, or a received one nothing was ever done to is ignored. Each subject's timelines come out as blocks of its kind — `_received_blocks(lines, subject)` (`on <subject> where …`, narrowed by `_QTY_COL[subject]` when symbols clash) and `_sent_blocks(lines, subject)` (`run` led by the sending verb with `_creator_terms`) — sent ones `expect`ing within a bound and ending `pass`, received ones `wait`ing; `_ANSWERS[subject]` gives the `when` rules (orders' requests, and an allocation's `replace`/`cancel` answered by `accept allocation`/`reject allocation`). `perform`'s event carries `detail.data` (the payload without `_` keys) and `detail.trade_before` for this: a correction is named by the terms the trade had. `stop(name, delays=)` writes the text, **event-triggered** (0.52, the user's call): every `heard` since the last action is written in order — `expect E within max(5 s, 3× the gap)` on the client side, `wait E` on the market side (a trailing wait nothing follows is dropped) — and the action follows with no delay; a market order's arrival counts as heard, so its first action has none; `after DELAY` appears only between two actions with nothing heard between them (under 50 ms dropped, the rest rounded by `_duration`). Market side, `_handlers`: a request kind (`cancel`/`replace`/`dk`) whose every occurrence was answered by the next step with the same verb and terms (`_ANSWERS`) leaves the main flow and becomes a top-of-block `when E` / answer — top-level handlers outlive the main flow (`listening`) — with `stop` after an accepted cancel that nothing was done after; one answered two ways, or left unanswered once, stays sequential. `delays=True` (the Stop dialog's and the editor prompt's "Keep my delays"; the timeline holds the timing either way) adds the think time: `after` between wait and action, and leading a handler's answer; `replace` keeps only the terms that differ from `prev`; codes become enum words; a client timeline that ends on something heard ends `pass`; trade targets resolve at stop against the order's trades stamped no later than the action. Market timelines with the same statements (delays aside) share a block (`symbol in […]`); a symbol in two groups gets `and order_qty == …`, narrower blocks first; client orders are one `run` block each, later ones led by `after OFFSET`. The output always checks clean (`test_macro_recorder.py` runs recordings against each other). Lists (0.78): a sent list's recorded orders are folded under its `new list` as `order` lines (`_list_orders`, `_members`), an `add order` step's order becomes its block (`_Timeline.children`), and a received list is born at `list` or `received` and narrowed by `order_count`. `MacroManager.record_start/record_stop/record_status` hold one recorder a side (`fix_cmd` commands of the same names); `stop()` ends them unwritten.

## From history: `history.py` (0.67)

`FromHistory(engine, side, delays=False).write(subject, row_ids)` is the recorder without the listening: it reads what was kept into the recorder's own `_Timeline`/`_Step` and the recorder's writer (`Recorder._write`, which `stop` also ends in) writes it, so a macro from history is the text a recording of the same thing would have been — `tests/test_macro_history.py::same` holds the two to each other line for line. **The messages are the source**, not the row's versions: `fix_messages` has every one as it crossed the wire with its stamp (`_seconds`/`_stamp`), and the versions (`<table>__history`, then the live row) only give the chain of IDs the row went by — `cl_ord_id`/`pending_cl_ord_id`/`orig_cl_ord_id` for an order, the ID, reference and `pending_alloc_id` columns for a family (`_FAMILY`).

- **Which messages.** An order's by `fix_messages.cl_ord_id IN chain`, plus the DontKnowTrades naming one of its reports' ExecIDs (a 35=Q names its trade, not the order); a family's by `instr(raw_message, SOH + "23=<id>" + SOH)` and the like, read and checked. `_life` then takes one life out of them: the message that began the row — the last beginning stamped before the row was written (`created_at`/`timestamp` + `_MARGIN`), since an ID comes round again and a row Message Replay's report created was written long after its order went out — and what followed it, up to the next beginning under the same ID.
- **Heard or done** is the direction: `RX` is heard (kept when the block's `_HEARD` names it), `TX` is an action. An ExecutionReport is classified by `report_kinds`, the way the runner hears one; sent, its kind is the verb (`_ORDER_ANSWERS`; a Canceled with 41 answers a request, without it is `unsol cxl`), and one the language has no verb for (pending, expired, done for day) goes to `left_out`, which the header lists (`# Left out — …`) and `from_history` answers.
- **Trades** are a model walked with the messages (`_Trades`), so a target is what the runner would have found at that moment — among the *live* trades, which the recorder's `_target` does not look at: after the first trade is busted the middle one is `first trade`. A re-notification is a fill, correction or bust on the wire; `_restated` tells it by the trade row's versions — a report whose row already said the same kind (`_REPORT_OF`) under the same ExecRefID.
- **Terms** come off the message: a replace's are the ones that differ from the terms last *accepted* (`accepted`, moved on by each `replaced` heard; `Recorder._terms` does the comparing), a family's through `ioi_columns`/`advert_columns`/`allocation_columns` into `_creator_terms`. `extra` is what the message carried beyond what the engine writes on one of its kind (`_OURS`), less the pairs the engine stamps the client with (`factory.client_fields`): the runner stamps it again.
- **An allocation's trade date** is written only when it was given: one equal to the day the message went out (`transact_time`, else `timestamp`) is the engine's default, and written down the macro would send that day's for ever (`_creator_terms`, so a recording leaves it out too, since 0.67).
- **A received IOI** gets what the recorder never writes: the orders sent against it (a sent 35=D carrying its ID in 23) as `new` lines of its `on ioi` block.
- `MacroManager.from_history(side, subject, rows, name=, save=, delays=)` (`fix_cmd macro_from_history`, the dialog's `row_ids`): a blank side is the side the first row is of; with `save` the name is checked before anything is read and nothing to write is an error — the dialog stays open on it. A row of the other side, a row that is gone and an empty list are refused.


## Lists from history (0.80)

`FromHistory._list(row)` reads a list's messages by `66=` (E/N/L/K/M/D) and its orders. Sent (`_sent_list`): the mode is the first message's (a later D makes the row `E+D`); each order's timeline (`_order`, `in_list_at` for one inside a NewOrderList, which has no D of its own) goes into `self.timelines` so `_list_orders` writes it as an `order` line — those whose D came later than `_QUIET` after the list are `add order` steps, their ListID stripped; L/K/M are `execute list`/`cancel list`/`request list status`, N is heard `accepted`/`rejected`/`executing`/`status`, and `done` is the first moment every order sent by then had finished (`_ENDS`). Received (`_received_list`): L/K/M heard; our N is `accept list`/`reject list` (Ack, ExecStarted, AllDone) or `list status` (text from 444), the automatic answer to an M skipped; our ERs to its orders sent together (within `_QUIET`) are `accept list` (acks), `fill all` (price when one), `cancel list orders` (canceled, no 41); orders worked one by one are left out and said. The recorder, for its part, puts an action before what its session heard while it was under way (`engine.performing` by session, `_Step.during`): an answer inside the send (mkfix answers a ListStatusRequest at once) is its answer.
